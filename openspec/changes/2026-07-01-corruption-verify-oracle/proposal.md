# Proposal: corruption-verify-oracle (core)

> Milestone: Cassandra Byte-for-Byte Parity Program (epic #966 / #974) — oracle-commissioning tooling.
> Issue: #1294 (follow-up from #1236). Routing: **re-routed design-driven** (owner decision 2026-07-01) —
> the deliverable is oracle/test-infra, but resolving where the CI drift guard lives is a **CI-tier design
> decision**, so the retained design is captured here and split from the tier work.
> Split partner: #1373 (acceptance #2 → epic #1360 nightly tier). Supersedes the two-mode PR-guard design
> that oscillated across 6 roborev rounds on PR #1337.

## Why

#1236 shipped a fail-closed corruption-verify parity test asserting CQLite `verify_sstable` verdicts match
captured Cassandra 5.0.2 `sstableverify` verdicts per corruption class. #1294 was the hardening follow-up
with two acceptance criteria:

1. **Bind the captured verdict to the FULL fixture directory**, not just the mutated component (a change to
   a copied non-mutated component could leave the bound hash unchanged while the captured verdict goes
   stale for the actual bytes under test).
2. **CI must not silently overwrite committed oracle edits** during pre-test regeneration.

Acceptance #1 is a well-scoped test-infra requirement and is **done and gate-PASS** on PR #1337.
Acceptance #2 is **not**: the corruption `.db` binaries are **gitignored** and absent from the fetch
bundle, so on a **pull_request** there are no committed bytes to validate the committed hash-bindings
against *before* regeneration. Every validation necessarily runs against regenerated bytes, and for the two
**non-byte-reproducible** BTI fixtures (`bti_partitions_footer_flip` → Partitions.db, `bti_rows_truncation`
→ Rows.db; both `verdict_byte_stable: no`) regenerated bytes legitimately differ from the committed hashes,
so a fatal PR-time guard **false-fails**. Rounds 1↔6 oscillated across exactly this design tension, and the
two guard steps in `compression-corruption-parity.yml` are the source of the two currently-red PR checks.

The tension is a **CI-tier question, not a test-authoring question**: epic #1360's target operating model
puts **regeneration parity in the Nightly deep tier, not per-PR**. Once the corruption lane runs nightly,
the committed-vs-generated drift guard is well-defined and fail-closed with no per-PR false-fails and no
need to commit binaries. That work is owned by **#1373** under epic #1360.

## What Changes

This change captures and lands the **converged core** of #1294 and removes the contested PR-time guard.

**In scope (this change / PR #1337):**

- **Full-fixture-directory verdict binding.** `generate-corruption-corpus.sh` records a deterministic
  hash of the whole fixture directory (`verdict_captured_for_dir_sha256`) alongside the existing mutated-
  component hash, and the parity test validates both against the on-disk fixtures — fatal for the
  byte-reproducible tree.
- **MODE-1 parity test** (`cqlite-core/tests/sstable_parity_corruption_verify.rs`): CQLite verify verdicts
  == captured Cassandra verdicts per corruption class, AND the committed byte-stable fixture tree matches
  its bound full-dir hash. Fail-closed on empty/absent fixtures.
- **Remove the PR-time committed-binding guard** (the two steps + `paths` additions in
  `compression-corruption-parity.yml`, and the CI-only guard scripts they call) — routed to #1373.

**Out of scope (routed to #1373 → epic #1360):**

- Moving the corruption regeneration lane to nightly + a PR smoke.
- The committed-vs-generated oracle drift guard running in the nightly regeneration lane, covering the two
  non-byte-reproducible BTI fixtures.

## Impact

- **Unblocks PR #1337**: removing the false-failing PR-time guard restores the two red checks to green;
  the retained core keeps the gate PASS.
- **No behavior change to CQLite's read/verify path** — this is test-infra + a dev-only `sha2` dependency.
- **No new committed binaries** — the fetch/gitignore model is unchanged.
- Acceptance #2 is preserved (not dropped) — it moves to the tier where it is well-defined (#1373).
